# GreyFinance three-minute demo

**Bitcoin treasury for teams.**

Show the working product and clearly identify the private Bitcoin test network. Do not present sample rows as settled transactions or builder wallets as independent users.

## 0:00–0:25 — The company workflow

“Small teams can pay with Bitcoin, but company spending needs more than a send button. Who requested it? Who approved it? Was it actually paid? GreyFinance makes that one clear workflow.”

Open the company overview. Point out the team, pending approvals, budget, and labeled test wallet.

## 0:25–0:55 — The company controls its wallet

Open wallet settings and choose the local Lightning test wallet. Explain that the same application supports a company-owned NWC wallet connection, restricted at the provider. The application receives no seed phrase.

Create a company receive invoice. Show its QR and pending state. Pay it from the second lab wallet and show the wallet-verified incoming receipt.

## 0:55–1:35 — An employee requests an expense

Switch to Employee. Show their own allowance and private request list, without the company treasury balance. Submit a fresh fixed-amount invoice generated by the second lab wallet.

“The server reads the amount and expiry from the invoice. This request cannot approve itself.”

Demonstrate that the employee cannot approve or execute the payment. Switch to Finance and review the request. Show the company and employee limit check, then approve it. The budget is reserved.

## 1:35–2:15 — Approve, confirm, verify

Open the explicit payment confirmation. Check requester, approver, amount, and irreversible payment notice. Pay once.

Show the settled state and durable receipt. Click **Verify receipt proof** so the browser independently checks the preimage against the payment hash. Open the audit record and ledger export.

“If a wallet response is unclear, GreyFinance holds the budget and checks status. It never repeats a payment just because the screen timed out.”

## 2:15–2:40 — Security and mobile usability

Show the 390px interface: clear navigation, readable request cards, labeled forms, and the same financial workflow. Mention independent approvals, role isolation, policy enforcement, and provider spending limits. Each identity can own one company and join other company workspaces.

## 2:40–3:00 — Impact and next step

“Our first users are small African studios and startups already using Bitcoin. The pilot goal is five teams and fifty expense requests per week. We will measure approval time, verified settlement, and how clearly people understand the controls.”

“The next iteration supports multiple owned companies with isolated wallets, fee accounting, recovery procedures, and an independent security review.”

Finish on a real verified test-network receipt. The source repository, setup instructions, architecture, security notes, tests, and honest limitations should be linked in the submission.
